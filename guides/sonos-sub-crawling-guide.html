Where to Put the Sonos Sub Mini in Small Apartments

1. The Apartment Dilemma: Mechanical Vibration vs. Air Pressure

Living in an apartment or flat with shared partition walls and lower-floor neighbors makes adding a subwoofer nerve-wracking. However, bass disturbance travels through two distinct paths:

  • Mechanical Conduction: The physical cabinet of a cheap or ported subwoofer shakes violently, transferring tactile vibrations directly through wooden floor joists and studs into adjoining rooms.
  • Acoustic Air Waves: Low-frequency soundwaves travel omnidirectionally through air, naturally penetrating thin drywall if bass resonance peaks at resonant frequencies.

The Sonos Sub Mini solves the first issue by design. Inside its sealed cylindrical chassis, two 6-inch woofers face each other directly across an open central tunnel. When firing, their physical kinetic energy cancels out completely (force-canceling architecture). You can balance a glass of water on top of the Sub Mini during an explosion scene and the water will barely ripple.

The remaining challenge in a small flat is managing the air waves—specifically preventing room modes and corner boundary gain.

2. The Corner Loading Trap: Why Your Bass Sounds Boomy

The most common instinct in a small room is to shove the Sub Mini into an unused 90-degree corner behind a credenza or plant.

In audio engineering, placing a low-frequency transducer into a corner is known as eighth-space loading. The corner acts as an acoustic horn, artificially boosting bass energy by up to +9 dB to +12 dB.

Why Artificial Corner Boost Is Bad in Flats:

  • It creates severe standing wave peaks between 40 Hz and 80 Hz, causing dialogue from your Beam or Ray soundbar to sound hollow and unintelligible.
  • Low-frequency resonance rings on, sustaining bass energy for hundreds of milliseconds longer than intended. This prolonged vibration penetrates party walls straight into neighboring flats.

3. How to Find the Sweet Spot: The Subwoofer Crawl

Instead of guessing where to place your Sub Mini, use the acoustic principle of reciprocity via the Subwoofer Crawl:

1

Place Sub Mini on Your Sofa Cushion

Disconnect the Sub Mini from the floor and temporarily set it right on the cushion where your head normally rests when watching TV.

2

Play a Track with Continuous Bass Notes

Loop a track featuring dynamic, melodic basslines across multiple octaves (e.g., electronic or acoustic jazz bass) at your standard listening volume.

3

Crawl the Perimeter on Hands & Knees

Keep your ears down at roughly 12 to 18 inches off the carpet or floorboards. Slowly crawl along the walls, beside your TV credenza, and next to your armchair.

4

Listen for Tight Definition, Not Sheer Volume

In some spots, the bass will sound bloated and boomy (acoustic peaks). In others, bass notes will disappear entirely (acoustic nulls). Look for the physical spot where each low note sounds tight, fast, and distinctly separated.

5

Swap Positions

Move the Sub Mini from your couch to that exact spot on the floor. Due to acoustic reciprocity, the bass will now sound identical when you sit back on the couch.

4. The 3 Best Physical Spots for Sub Mini in Small Flats

Top Choice

Flanking the Media Stand

Place the Sub Mini 6 to 12 inches to the left or right of your TV console, at least 4 inches away from the rear wall. This provides clean forward staging that integrates seamlessly with your Beam or Ray.

Near-Field

Beside the Sofa / End Table

Placing the sub right next to your primary seating spot delivers immediate tactile impact at far lower overall volume, keeping low frequencies contained to your ears rather than vibrating neighboring units.

Console Alcove

Inside Open Media Bays

Because the Sub Mini does not vibrate, it can sit inside open furniture bays. Just ensure the central tunnel has at least 2 inches of clearance at both ends so the opposing drivers breathe freely.

5. Decoupling & Sonos App Settings for Shared Walls

To ensure peak clarity and neighbor-friendly acoustics, combine physical isolation with calibrated software trims:

Acoustic Decoupling Isolation Pad

Essential for Hardwood

While the Sub Mini cabinet is mechanically inert, placing a dense high-density foam or rubber isolation pad underneath absorbs secondary acoustic floor resonance, preventing low frequencies from exciting hollow laminate or floorboards.

Recommended Sonos App Settings in Flats:

  • Sub Audio Level: Keep between 0 and -2. Never boost Sub Audio above +2 in small rooms; it overwhelms the soundbar crossover.
  • Night Sound (Moon Icon): Enable during late hours when watching films. This compresses the dynamic range, suppressing explosions while boosting quiet dialogue.
  • Sub Phase: Toggle between 0° and 180° in the Sonos app while seated on your sofa. Whichever setting sounds punchier and louder is the phase in sync with your soundbar.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I lay the Sonos Sub Mini on its side?

No. Unlike the full-size Sonos Sub (Gen 3/4) which has flat rubber feet and can lay horizontally under a raised sofa, the Sub Mini must stand upright on its circular base. Laying it sideways blocks the force-canceling tunnel and damages the driver suspension over time.

Should I use Trueplay after positioning my Sub Mini?

Yes. Trueplay is essential after running the Subwoofer Crawl. It analyzes the acoustic crossover frequency between your soundbar (e.g., Beam or Ray) and the Sub Mini, flattening low-frequency room mode spikes caused by small apartment drywall.
